## 6.1 Verification Execution and Coverage

Technical verification was conducted using a headless semantic implementation of the specified village-service fixture. The final assessed build was UAIS-HEADLESS-0.1.2, using UAIS-VRI-1.0, ServiceVersion 1.0.0, simulator UAIS-LEDGER-0.1.0 and test dataset UAIS-EXEC-DATA-0.1.1. The governing specifications remained UAIS-S3-FINAL-1.0, UAIS-CAT-1.0 and VER-CAT1. The final execution, identified as 20260913T022729Z-05c9476b, took place on 13 September 2026 UTC. Versioned manifests and SHA-256 fingerprints were frozen before the corresponding runs. Existing expected objects were preserved across implementation revisions; six additional instances were introduced in a separately versioned dataset.

The implementation executed classification functions, scoped mapping gates, catalogue adapters, selected resolver comparisons, state transitions and a deterministic transaction ledger. It represented U and S in memory and produced machine-readable observation, decision and execution records. It did not render a browser interface or connect to a government service. Functional display, capability, semantic-equivalence and several action-specific predicates were supplied as controlled witnesses. Consequently, the execution tested the model's use of these witnesses rather than the correctness of their acquisition from a rendered application.

The final inventory contained 775 entries: 770 executable deterministic instances and five explicit not-run coverage entries. All 770 executed instances matched their frozen expected objects; none produced an unresolved execution or a final object mismatch. Agreement was therefore 770/770 for this selected inventory. This percentage is not a claim that the complete framework or every VER-CAT1 branch has passed. In particular, catalogue enumeration checks and rule-family filter projections are narrower than end-to-end tests of their corresponding action or rule family.

Table 10 reports the nonexclusive oracle breakdown. O1 had 54 executed instances, O2 641, O3 44, O4 38, O5 42 and end-to-end verification 14. A case could be tagged with several oracles, so these row totals must not be added to obtain a unique case count. The final package associated at least one executed instance with each of the 35 historical parent identifiers and 44 current child-family identifiers. This association provides identifier-level traceability; it does not demonstrate complete parameter, scope, guard or effect coverage within each family.

Two additional acceptance audits examined requirements insufficiently assessed by the initial harness. Eight representative traces and five transaction branches were assessed separately, with their earlier failures retained. These audits overlap the executable scenarios and are not pooled into the 770-case denominator. No participant study, usability measurement or cognitive outcome was included.

## 6.2 Classification and Decision Verification

The 54 O1 instances comprised 39 direct classification checks and 15 assistance/connectivity sequence cases. On the supplied inputs, the recorded outcomes agreed with the expected recency classifications, continuous ISS dimension values and missing-dimension handling, device descriptors, reflow/feature witness classifications, response-time classes, logical-chain stability and factual vector representations. The six exact response-time inputs 1.999, 2.000, 2.001, 3.999, 4.000 and 4.001 s were classified as Preferred, Acceptable, Acceptable, Acceptable, Poor and Poor, respectively. Timeout was classified as Poor, unavailable timing as Unknown, and the tested validation-rejection response did not independently create a connectivity failure.

The assistance sequences distinguished preview exploration from typed requests, retained the requested need kind through recovery, and did not convert generic help into all six assistance-derived needs. Tested duplicate events, a sequence gap, excluded duration/error events, unsafe resumption and a background-origin resumption event produced the expected sequence outputs. Connectivity sequences reproduced the tested AtRisk/Disrupted classifications, clean recovery, recovery interrupted by unknown evidence and relapse. These are exact agreements for the supplied event lists; they do not establish exhaustive state-machine coverage or the reliability of a real event collector.

O2 included 252 C→N instances: each of the 84 matrix cells was evaluated under true, false and unknown relevance witnesses. Thus all 51 non-dash relationships were represented under the three witness conditions, and the dash relationships remained inactive. The N→R checks comprised 112 instances, covering each of the 28 cells under true, false, unknown and inactive-need conditions. Twenty-four additional checks exercised the six current assistance edges across the four control states. Score-only and descriptor-only nonactivation checks were also included.

These mapping results verify topology and three-valued gating at a declared predicate boundary. The implementation accepts several base and task-relevance predicates as inputs; it does not infer all those predicates from raw interaction evidence. A successful gate comparison therefore cannot be interpreted as proof that every natural-language mapping condition has been independently operationalised and verified.

For the 24 actions, 144 gate instances examined an eligible target, an exclusion, a named temporary blocker, an unknown witness, an illegal variant and absence of a mediated need. The expected READY/KEEP, BLOCKED/SUPPRESS and WAIT-or-UNKNOWN/DEFER outcomes were obtained. An additional 67 legal-value membership checks and ten variant-registry checks confirmed the tested catalogue entries. These membership and gate checks do not establish that every realisation produces all declared content, service and accessibility effects. Full concrete scope/effect verification remains an explicit coverage gap.

The sixteen rule-mask cases checked a family-filter projection. They are not evidence of sixteen fully justified end-to-end multi-need scenarios. The final dataset also included explicit rejection of adaptive query/retry candidates lacking the required nominated rule. Table 10 separates these bounded decision checks from an unsupported assertion of complete action correctness.

## 6.3 Resolver Behaviour

The 44 O3 instances examined fixed alternative sets, selected compatible compositions and scoped reductions. With all higher predicates established in the supplied plans, strict-superset need coverage prevailed over a lower change count. Continuity and stability sets were compared before minimal change. Non-nested coverage sets were retained for lower-criterion comparison; where a lower criterion selected a unique survivor, that survivor was selected. Final substantive incomparability produced DEFER rather than arbitrary canonical selection. Unknown required comparison evidence did not become a genuine tie.

The tested cases also distinguished READY from WAIT, BLOCKED and UNKNOWN, and checked selected cases in both input orders. This permutation check provides evidence for those alternative sets, not a general proof over arbitrary plan cardinalities. Canonical selection was exercised only in the implementation's restricted simple-key domain. The complete typed tuple, exact-decimal and declared-set canonical normalisation required by RES-CD1 was not implemented or executed and remains open.

Table 11 presents representative compositions. The model retained the compatible term-explanation/autosave pair, selected the registered cue/progress representation for matching identities, used contextual guidance for the supplied guidance/density conflict, and admitted the total within-stage grouping partition. It rejected the incomplete partition. A registered text-equivalence witness permitted the example conversion, whereas false or unavailable equivalence did not establish preservation. The scoped reduction cases retained protected elements, selected a nonempty eligible subset when available and suppressed a reduction with no eligible subset.

These outcomes demonstrate the selected composition branches under supplied compatibility and content witnesses. They do not show that a browser can render those combinations without loss, or that the configured representations produce an accessibility benefit. The implementation also does not yet cover the complete semantic effect and normalisation domain required for framework-wide resolver verification.

## 6.4 Stateful and Invariant Verification

The state scenarios exercised ordinary application, configuration equality, active-input deferral, a stale snapshot and rollback after an injected application failure. In the active-input model, an unfinished FLD05 buffer, selection and composition flag were retained while structural application was deferred. The deferred trace scenarios recorded fresh successor decisions after the modeled safe-point event, including execution and supersession branches. These results concern the modeled state; browser blur, unmounting, reordering and actual IME behaviour were not observed.

One explicitly stale configuration decision was prevented from committing. Separate response-boundary cases rejected a stale service response and accepted a matching current response without altering the simulator's committed-effect count. These are distinct checks: a stale response rejection is not counted as an additional stale configuration decision prevention.

Table 12 reports 30 logged admission-level evaluations of each of the five local invariant predicates. No false or unknown predicate was recorded on an admitted transition. The negative controls intentionally proposed changed field values, logical position, input state, transaction state or required-route reachability. Their expected false predicates were detected; these controls did not apply the corrupt proposals and are not counted as observed adaptation-induced violations. A separate modeled hide/reappearance sequence preserved the conditional FLD05 value.

The local checks cover recorded field/state equality and the declared semantic reachability graph. They do not constitute exhaustive enforcement of all fixture validation, authorisation, asynchronous interleaving or browser reachability requirements. Thus the observed violation count is zero within these checks, rather than evidence of unconditional invariant preservation by a complete application.

## 6.5 Transaction-Recovery Verification

The five TX-1 branches were executed separately against the deterministic ledger. Each branch included the initial simulated submission attempt, an ambiguous client-known state, a need-mediated query decision, a read-only status request and a subsequent decision. The tests also examined repeated permit use, duplicate query tokens, query-origin self-trigger exclusion, independent fresh query tokens, and wrong-identity or stale responses. Table 13 distinguishes submission attempts, logical transaction identities and committed effects.

X-C retained its existing committed effect and issued no retry. X-P issued no retry and retained pending feedback. X-R issued one permitted same-identity retry, giving two submission attempts in total and one committed effect. Its feedback described the outstanding retry as pending rather than inferring an unobserved completion response. X-F suppressed retry and recorded terminal-rejection feedback. X-U issued no retry and recorded unknown feedback. The query itself made no ledger mutation. No duplicate committed effect was observed in the tested branches and duplicate-permit checks.

An intermediate acceptance audit found defects that the initial status/count oracle had missed. In build 0.1.1, the completed and terminal-rejected branches supplied DEFER rather than SUPPRESS for the retry target, and none of the five branches recorded the required feedback output. All five branch assessments therefore failed their stricter TX-1 acceptance check. Build 0.1.2 supplied a known-negative retry guard for terminal states and recorded mandatory baseline status feedback. The five branches then passed the same acceptance expectations. Both the failing and retested records are retained.

The feedback evidence is a machine-readable service output with baseline-safety provenance, not a rendered panel or an independently exercised adaptive R3.A5 realisation. These results support the checked ledger/orchestration branches. They do not establish production backend reliability, distributed idempotency, or complete recovery behaviour for every service operation.

## 6.6 Traceability and End-to-End Results

Eight representative traces covered applied, composed, transformed, suppressed, deferred-then-executed, deferred-then-superseded, unknown-evidence and transaction-reconciliation scenarios. The original stage-presence checker accepted all eight, but a stricter TRACE-CD1/TRACE-CAT1 audit identified missing version linkage, resolver-plan witnesses, commit-token evidence and deferred lifecycle information. All eight failed that initial required-field acceptance audit. This exposed both an instrumentation defect and an insufficient harness check.

The revised implementation recorded the missing fields and lifecycle changes, and the checker inspected those requirements. All eight representative traces passed the final structural acceptance audit, as shown in Table 14. Each contained the ten required stages and their linked predecessors. Three negative controls independently removed a version field, a commit token or a parent reference; all three altered traces were rejected. The initial eight failures remain in the evidence package and are not replaced by the retest.

The final result establishes reconstructability of the checked model records and required fields in these scenarios. It does not independently validate the truth of every supplied semantic witness, prove complete trace coverage for all action scopes, or establish that a deployed collector would preserve these records under concurrency or storage failure.

## 6.7 Verification Summary

The final build agreed with its 770 selected deterministic expected objects, and the separate final acceptance checks passed for eight representative traces and five transaction branches. Three implementation/harness defects were discovered and corrected within the tested scope. Thirteen failed acceptance assessments across the earlier trace and transaction audits are preserved with their defect and retest links. No contradiction in the governing specification was identified during this work; this is a discovery count, not proof that the specification contains no undiscovered defect.

The evidence supports selected classification, mapping-gate, resolver, state and transaction behaviour in a controlled semantic model. It does not yet support the entire requested verification domain. Five explicit gaps remain: observed browser reflow, complete concrete action-contract effects/scopes, full canonical comparison normalisation, independently justified end-to-end scenarios for all rule masks, and actual browser/IME preservation. The remaining concrete subcase space has not been completely enumerated, so the five gap entries must not be interpreted as exactly five remaining executable tests.

Accordingly, Section 6 should remain provisional. The present execution provides reproducible bounded evidence and documented defect correction, but the full verification claim should not be locked until the outstanding implementation and coverage obligations are addressed. No conclusion about improved comprehension, cognitive load, usability or cognitive-accessibility effectiveness follows from these technical results.
