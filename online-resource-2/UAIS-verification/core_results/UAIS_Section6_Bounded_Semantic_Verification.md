# 6 Verification and Results

## 6.1 Verification Execution and Coverage

The framework was evaluated through bounded technical and behavioural verification of a headless semantic model. The evaluated domain comprised controlled context and task-relevance witnesses, scoped need/rule decisions, representative action effects, concurrent adaptation scenarios, resolver comparisons, and selected stateful service interactions. Browser-based context acquisition, rendering and empirical user outcomes were outside the evaluated domain.

The final build, UAIS-CORE-0.2.2, used UAIS-S3-FINAL-1.0, UAIS-CAT-1.0, VER-CAT1 and the UAIS-VRI-1.0 service fixture. Its dataset was UAIS-CORE-DATA-1.2. The closure run 20260913T030322Z-8f183b76 and regression run 20260913T030324Z-075d96fc were executed on 13 September 2026 UTC. Each build's implementation, fixture, input and expectation files were fingerprinted in an execution manifest before execution. The previous 770 executable case identities and expected outcomes were retained without alteration.

The final evaluated inventory contained 868 unique executable instances: 770 regression cases and 98 closure additions. All 868 matched their expected outcomes, with no failed or unresolved instance in the final declared executable domain. No previously executable case was removed. This agreement applies to the selected instances, not to every possible action combination, parameter value, service workflow or browser implementation.

The closure additions comprised 49 action-related instances, 27 canonical-key/resolver instances, 21 scoped multi-rule scenarios and one query-provenance check. The action-related instances comprised 24 positive action cases, all ten declared transformation variants, fourteen unlisted-transform attempts for actions with empty transformation lists, and an additional draft-restoration case. Table 10 reports the revised nonexclusive oracle coverage: O1 54, O2 712, O3 116, O4 85, O5 112 and end-to-end verification 71 executed instances. Because cases can address several oracles, these row counts are not additive.

The five original gap records remain in the historical inventory with their original not-run labels. The closure evidence discharges three at a declared representative semantic level; browser acquisition and real DOM/IME behaviour are retained as explicit boundaries. These historical category records are neither deleted nor counted as five unfinished executable tests within the revised domain. The earlier failing runs and their retests also remain available.

## 6.3 Canonical Normalisation and Concurrent Resolver Behaviour

The canonical key represents the sorted action tuple sequence comprising catalogue version, ActionID, VariantID, scope and typed parameters. The implementation normalises Unicode strings to NFC, distinguishes boolean and enum domains, compares finite decimals exactly without binary-floating-point conversion, preserves ordered sequences, normalises declared unordered sets and recursively processes typed objects. Equivalent decimal coefficients are normalised without context-dependent rounding. Parameter-name and action-tuple ordering is deterministic.

The 27 canonical/resolver checks covered equivalent and unequal exact decimals, numeric ordering, signed zero, NFC-equivalent and distinct strings, booleans and enums, ordered collections, reordered sets, nested objects, multiple action tuples, prefix ordering and candidate permutations. Binary-float, unavailable and nonfinite decimal inputs did not establish an admissible exact key. Synthetic typed values exercise the normalisation utility; they do not extend the legal parameter domain of any catalogue action.

Canonical selection occurred in the tested genuinely equal semantic comparisons. Unknown required evidence and final substantive incomparability produced DEFER without canonical selection. Where a lower criterion distinguished survivors with non-nested coverage sets, selection followed that criterion rather than relabelling their earlier relationship as equality. Table 12 records expected and observed outcomes.

An additional equivalent-plan audit exposed an implementation defect in build 0.2.0. Although differently represented equal decimals produced equal keys, duplicate semantic plans were not merged, and their selected record identity could depend on input order. Both audited permutations failed. The correction retained all source aliases through COMBINE while selecting a single record for the identical effect. Both permutations passed in the final build. Choosing a representative record alias does not add a seventh preference between different adaptation effects.

The multi-rule closure replaced the former mask-only projection with independently scoped evidence. A term-clarification request justified N1→R1 while its optional simplification edge was false. Separate reminder scopes justified grouping support through N4→R2, interrupted draft-reference continuity through N4→R3, and retained staged references with legal back/review access through N4→R4. Each conditional relationship had its own frozen object-level demand and preservation witnesses; rules were obtained by evaluating the mapping rather than assigning a requested mask.

The sixteen scenarios generated the empty set and all fifteen nonempty rule subsets, including four-rule concurrency. No requested subset was unreachable under these declared scoped inputs. Five additional scenarios examined suppression of an invalid grouping target, a permitted grouping transformation, structural deferral during an unfinished edit, lower-criterion resolution of incomparable coverage, and final incomparability with safe hold. Table 13 records the context/need scopes, nominations, concrete actions and dispositions. The competing-plan cases use an explicit controlled feasibility witness for an exclusive support region; they do not imply that the involved actions always conflict.

Candidate generation preceded application, and the admitted actions retained their independent need provenance. The selected property effects and preserved semantic state agreed with the expected outcomes across these scenarios. This closes the independent multi-rule gap at the declared scenario level, while leaving exhaustive combinations of the 24 actions outside the claim.

## 6.4 Stateful Behaviour and Invariant Preservation

The complete regression retained the earlier application, same-U, active-input deferral, stale-decision, rollback, restoration and response-boundary cases. The additional action cases checked source values, logical position, valid progress, input state, required semantic routes and transaction identity at the configuration boundary. Command effects and subsequently accepted service evidence were evaluated separately from that configuration commit.

In the declared cases, no configuration-boundary invariant violation was observed. The previously tested stale configuration decision remained prevented from committing, and response races remained separate from configuration changes. The draft-restoration case distinguished preservation of already entered values from an authorised restoration event that adds a saved value. Reconciliation likewise distinguished unchanged configuration-time transaction truth from a later accepted status response.

During the concurrent deferral scenario, the model retained the unfinished field buffer, selection and composition representation while withholding the structural target. These are symbolic state-preservation results. They do not show preservation of actual DOM nodes, focus, browser selection, composition events or IME behaviour. Similarly, semantic reachability witnesses establish behaviour given the modeled required routes, rather than proving that a browser observer identifies every inaccessible route.

## 6.5 Transaction-Recovery Verification

The final regression preserved the five TX-1 branch results and the duplicate-token, response-identity and service-event boundaries. The independent transaction acceptance audit again passed all five branches under the same criteria. Completed and terminal-rejected status suppressed retry. Pending and unknown status withheld retry. Safely retryable evidence admitted at most one same-identity attempt under its current permit and a fresh decision.

The distinction between attempts, identities and committed effects was maintained. X-C retained one effect already committed before the lost response and issued no new retry. X-R had two submission attempts in total, one logical identity and one committed effect. The remaining branches issued no retry. Read-only queries added no committed effect, and no duplicate committed effect was observed in the selected ledger cases. Following retry dispatch, recorded feedback remained pending rather than claiming an unobserved completion response.

The earlier transaction acceptance failures remain in the package: two terminal branches initially used the wrong retry disposition, and feedback was absent in all five branch records. The corrected outputs were retested without changing the TX-1 acceptance expectations. The closure additionally checks representative adaptive action effects, including explicit submission feedback that reads S without querying, retrying or mutating transaction truth.

The ledger is a deterministic single-process fixture. These observations do not establish production-backend reliability, distributed idempotency or the behaviour of an official government transaction.

Although expectations were separately frozen and were not generated by asking the implementation for its output, this does not provide the assurance of an independent external implementation or reviewer. The source, inputs, expected objects, observations and retest history are retained to support such inspection.

## 6.7 Verification Summary and Claim Boundary

The closure supports bounded semantic-model technical and behavioural verification of the declared framework instantiation. The final build matched 868 selected expected outcomes, comprising 770 unchanged regression cases and 98 additions. Representative effects were checked for all 24 actions, all ten declared transformation variants were exercised, the full typed canonical-key utility was tested, and independently justified scenarios represented all sixteen rule subsets and selected concurrent dispositions.

The three core gaps are therefore closed to the declared representative contract and scenario level. Browser/reflow acquisition and actual DOM/IME preservation remain explicit boundaries. Where raw context or task-relevance predicates are supplied as witnesses, the evidence establishes subsequent gating and behaviour given those inputs, not complete derivation from natural interaction evidence.

The results do not establish complete implementation verification, exhaustive state-space or action-combination coverage, production deployment readiness, or improved UX, comprehension, cognitive load, usability or cognitive-accessibility effectiveness. Within this stated boundary, Section 6 is supported by the retained execution evidence.
